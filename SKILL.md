# PhD Supervisor Outreach

Prepare one carefully researched supervisor email at a time. The result should sound like a trained student who has read the professor's work and wants to learn, not like a bulk template or a research-plan introduction.

## Gather the case

When working inside this repository, read the relevant file under `cases/`. Otherwise, use the professor information and candidate materials supplied in the conversation.

Before drafting, verify:

- the professor's identity, current institution, department, and role;
- the target PhD programme and intake year;
- an official profile or another reliable identity source;
- any candidate-specific claim that will appear in the email.

If the professor is ambiguous or a required fact is missing, ask one concise clarification question. Do not guess.

## Load the required guidance

Read [references/candidate_profile.md](references/candidate_profile.md) before using the candidate's background, techniques, results, or mechanistic interpretations.

Read [WORKFLOW.md](WORKFLOW.md) before drafting. Follow its research check, three writing passes, output format, and final checklist.

Read supporting CVs, manuscripts, transcripts, and other files only when the current case names or supplies them.

## Research the supervisor

Use current sources. Prioritize the official university profile, PubMed, the university publication page, and recent original papers. Prefer work from the latest three to five years.

Do not rely on titles or keywords. For the paper used in the email, understand the biological question, model, central result, strength of the proposed mechanism, limitation or open question, and the genuine connection to the candidate.

Center the email on one relevant original paper. Add one or at most two specific observations or questions. Confirm that the paper has not already answered the proposed question.

If current research sources cannot be accessed, state the limitation and request suitable links or PDFs instead of fabricating depth.

## Write the email

Apply all three passes in `WORKFLOW.md`:

1. Build an evidence-based first draft.
2. Rewrite it after a deeper paper check.
3. Polish it into a natural, academic student voice.

Use short, varied sentences. Avoid generic praise, exaggerated fit claims, dense mechanism summaries, technique lists, and formulaic transitions. Preserve scientific uncertainty. Never turn an association into a proven causal chain.

The final email must contain six natural body paragraphs and be delivered as a copy-ready English version followed by a faithful, natural Chinese version. Keep citations and research notes outside the email.

When operating in this repository, save:

- `outputs/<case-slug>_research_notes.md`
- `outputs/<case-slug>_email.md`

When no repository workspace is available, present the verified research basis briefly and then provide the final bilingual email directly in the conversation.

Do not send an email, contact a professor, or publish repository changes unless the user explicitly asks for that separate action.
